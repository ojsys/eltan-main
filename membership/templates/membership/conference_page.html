Preamble

In an era of rapid globalization and technological advancements, the landscape of English Language Teaching (ELT) in Nigeria is evolving. Traditional methods of instruction, while foundational, must be reimagined to meet the diverse linguistic, cultural, and technological realities of today’s learners. This conference, themed "Innovative ELT Pedagogy in Nigeria," seeks to explore forward-thinking approaches that enhance language proficiency, foster critical thinking, and improve learning outcomes in classrooms across the country.

Conference Sub-themes:

  1. Enhancing Teacher Wellbeing for Effective ELT
  2. Teachers as Behavioral Change Agents in ELT
  3. Technology Integration for Innovative ELT Pedagogy
  4. Culturally Responsive ELT in Multilingual Classrooms
  5. Building 21st-Century Skills Through ELT
  6. Gender and Inclusion in ELT Practices
  7. Community Engagement Through ELT Pedagogy
  8. Sustainable Policies for Innovative ELT Practices
  9. Exploring Nigerian English in ELT Pedagogy
  10. Teaching Syntax (Grammar) for Academic Writing Proficiency
  11. Pragmatics and Communication Skills in ELT
  12. Phonology Instruction for Improved Spoken English
  13. Integrating Literature into ELT
  14. Analyzing ELT Implications of Nigerian Linguistic Diversity
  15. Collaborative Classrooms for Engaging ELT Experience
  16. Introducing Innovative ELT Practices for Global Competitiveness
  17. Integrating Storytelling into ELT Practices
  18. ELT Practices for Improved Performance in External Examinations
  19. Functional Grammar and ELT
  20. Any other relevant topics in Formal Linguistics, English, and Literature relating such to English language pedagogy

Through discussions on cutting-edge methodologies, digital integration, learner-centered strategies, and context-responsive teaching practices, this gathering will bring together educators, researchers, and policymakers to reimagine ELT in Nigeria. By embracing innovation, we aim to bridge existing gaps, empower educators, and equip students with the linguistic skills necessary to thrive in an increasingly interconnected world.

Join us as we engage in insightful conversations, share best practices, and chart a new course for English Language Teaching in Nigeria. Click Here To Submit Your Abstract

Abstract Submission Guidelines

General Guidelines

  • • Title: 10 words maximum (Capitalize the initials of content words only)
  • • Abstract: 250 words maximum
  • • Citations: APA (7th edition) or MLA (9th edition) style
  • • Main text and abstract must be in Times New Roman, font size 12
  • • Include full name, institutional affiliation, and contact details
  • • Registration required per presenter
  • • Abstract submission deadline: May 30, 2025, by 21:00 GMT
  • • Abstracts not submitted by May 30, 2025, will not be considered for the printed Book of Abstracts
  • • All Virtual presentations will be a post-conference activity via zoom
  • • All the PowerPoint slides for Virtual Presentations must be submitted by August 20, 2025 to eltanconfasaba@gmail.com

Note: The LOC will organize a session to help especially first-time presenters and anyone else who might be interested to learn how to write successful abstracts.

Abstract Evaluation Rubric

Criterion 0 1 2 3
Relevance and currency of the topic The topic is not current and the content of the abstract would not be of interest to either English teachers or researchers. The topic is somehow current and the content of the abstract might be of interest to either English teachers or researchers. The topic is current and the content of the abstract would be of definite interest to either English teachers or researchers. The topic is very current and the content of the abstract would be of interest to both English teachers and researchers.
Clarity of objectives and findings The objectives of the paper are not clearly stated or implied. The objectives of the paper allow for a general understanding but outcomes are implied but not explicitly stated, or may be too general or broad to be achievable. The objectives of the paper allow for a specific understanding of the topic and outcomes are stated or implied but may lack sufficient focus. The objectives of the paper are clear and achievable within the scope of the topic and specific expected outcomes of the presentation.
Evidence of theory-practice link There is no theory-practice link. The abstract lacks a clear link between theory and practice. There is some evidence of research/theory support. There is a strong and relevant theory/research as evidence/support.
Quality of abstract as indicator of presentation quality The language used in the abstract suggests that the presentation may be poor. The language used in the abstract suggests that the presentation may be uneven or of moderate quality. The language used in the abstract suggests that the presentation will be of an acceptable quality. The abstract is well written and suggests that the presentation will be of a good quality.
